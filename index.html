Live Gaussian-Splat Volumetric Capture
from a 10-Camera RGB-D Rig

Performers fused, rendered and recorded as Gaussian splats at 30 fps, with no per-scene training, and exported as compact splat sequences.

Abdul Rehman
French Touch Factory · ITHACA
Paper (soon) Video Code (private)
TL;DR Depth-sensor fusion → surface-aligned Gaussians with full-resolution colour → 30 fps live render and 30 fps SPZ sequence recording (.gseq) that plays back in Unity.

Abstract

Most Gaussian-splatting pipelines optimise each scene or each frame offline, which takes minutes to hours. We instead build Gaussians directly from a calibrated multi-camera RGB-D rig, frame by frame, in real time. Depth from ten sensors is fused into a persistent, confidence-weighted surface model. Each surface element becomes a Gaussian aligned to the local surface normal, with its true projected size and colour gathered from the full-resolution colour images of all cameras, including a per-splat colour slope. The live result renders in Unity at 30 fps and records to a compact sequence of standard SPZ frames that plays back next to optimised 3DGS environments.

This is real-time Gaussian-splat volumetric capture: fusion drawn as Gaussians, not optimised 3DGS. Quality versus offline 3DGS / 4DGS on held-out cameras is still to be measured.

Recording and playback

  • One .gseq file per take: a small header and index, then one standard SPZ frame per capture frame.
  • Paced recording, so no source frame is skipped (63 / 63).
  • Y-up export; plays in Unity next to optimised 3DGS environments.

Limitations

  • Not optimised 3DGS: detail is limited by depth-sensor quality and fusion resolution; hair, hands, dark or shiny clothes and fast motion are the hardest cases.
  • Areas no camera sees (behind a held instrument, between the legs) have no splats.
  • Several thresholds are tuned for this rig and need re-tuning for other rigs or sensors.
  • No held-out-camera evaluation (PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS) against offline methods yet.

Next

  • Held-out-camera evaluation of the live result as the baseline.
  • Learned refiner: a small network that corrects each fused Gaussian (position, scale, opacity, colour), trained by rendering into hidden cameras and comparing with their photos, then run live in Unity.
  • Orbbec sensors as the Azure Kinect replacement.
